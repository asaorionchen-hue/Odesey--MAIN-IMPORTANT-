# Odyssey Project: Scene & Quote Research List

This document lists the current scenes and embedded quotes from the game project, mapped to the corresponding books of the *Odyssey*. Use this list to verify translation accuracy, historical context, and to identify potential areas for expanded dialogue or visual detail.

## Book 5: Ogygia (Calypso’s Island)

### Scenes
- **Ogygia Shore**: Odysseus is found weeping on the beach, gazing at the sea. Hermes arrives to deliver Zeus's command for his release.
- **Calypso’s Cave**: The interior of the cave with four springs and lush vegetation. Calypso offers immortality to Odysseus.
- **Raft Building**: Odysseus spends four days building a raft from twenty trees.

### Key Quotes
> "He sat on the rocks and wept, as always, weeping, his eyes never dry, his sweet life ebbing away with longing for home. The nymph no longer pleased him." (OgygiaShoreScene.ts)

> "Zeus commands you to send him home. His fate is not to die here. It is his destiny to see his family and reach home." — **Hermes** (OgygiaShoreScene.ts)

> "I saved him. I fed him. I promised to make him immortal. But if Zeus commands, I will send him." — **Calypso** (OgygiaShoreScene.ts)

> "Goddess, do not be angry at me for this. I know that my wise Penelope is less than you in beauty and stature... but even so I wish and long every day to reach my home." — **Odysseus** (CalypsoCaveScene.ts)

## Book 11: Nekyia (The Underworld)

### Scenes
- **Trench Scene**: Odysseus performs the sacrifice at the edge of the world. He meets the shades of Elpenor, Tiresias, and his mother Anticlea.
- **Parade of Shades**: A sequence where Odysseus witnesses the souls of famous women from Greek mythology.

### Key Quotes
> "The souls of the dead came swarming up from Erebus — brides, young men, old men who had suffered much, tender girls with hearts new to sorrow." (TrenchScene.ts)

> "Burn me with my armor. Heap up a mound for me on the grey sea shore. Plant my oar upon the tomb — the oar I used to row when I was alive with my companions." — **Elpenor** (TrenchScene.ts)

> "You seek a honey-sweet homecoming, shining Odysseus, but a god will make it hard for you. I do not think Poseidon will let go of his anger — he is furious because you blinded his son." — **Tiresias** (TrenchScene.ts)

> "And it was longing for you, my brilliant Odysseus, your gentle ways, your kind heart — that took away my sweet life." — **Anticlea** (TrenchScene.ts)

> "Three times I moved towards her, and my heart urged me to embrace her. Three times she fluttered out of my arms like a shadow or a dream." — **Odysseus** (TrenchScene.ts)

## Book 12: Sirens, Scylla, and Charybdis

### Scenes
- **Circe’s Warning**: Circe provides the roadmap for the dangers ahead.
- **Strait Approach**: The ship approaches the island of the Sirens.
- **Scylla’s Passage / Charybdis**: Navigation through the narrow strait between the six-headed monster and the whirlpool.

### Key Quotes
> "First you will come to the Sirens. Plug your men's ears with wax. If you want to hear them, have your men tie you to the mast." — **Circe** (CirceWarningScene.ts)

> "Under the other cliff is Charybdis — she swallows the sea three times a day. Not even Poseidon could save you. Hug Scylla's cliff. Better to lose six men than everyone." — **Circe** (CirceWarningScene.ts)

> "Stubborn man. Scylla is not mortal. She is a deathless evil. Running is your only option. Do not stop to fight or she will take six more." — **Circe** (CirceWarningScene.ts)

## Books 21-23: The Return to Ithaca

### Scenes
- **Courtyard / Palace Exterior**: Odysseus (in disguise) returns to his home.
- **Great Hall (The Trial of the Bow)**: Odysseus strings the great bow, shoots an arrow through twelve axe-heads, and begins the slaughter of the suitors.
- **Odysseus’s Bedroom**: The final reunion with Penelope, featuring the secret of the bed carved from a living olive tree.

### Key Quotes
> "Odysseus strings the great bow. The axes fall silent. He has come home." (BedroomScene.ts)

> "Then the resourceful Odysseus, as soon as he had lifted the great bow and checked it all over... as a man skilled in the lyre and in song easily stretches a string over a new peg... so without effort Odysseus strung the great bow." — **Book 21** (Context for GreatHallScene.ts)

> "The suitors were struck with fear, and their skin changed color... Zeus thundered aloud, showing a sign." — **Book 21** (Context for GreatHallScene.ts)
